One-Pot Creamy Beef Pasta Bake

Recipes · 14 min read · Updated Published: Jul 30, 2026 · Updated: Jul 30, 2026

⚡ Quick summary

This creamy beef pasta bake delivers a comforting, high-protein dinner cooked entirely in a single skillet in just 40 minutes. By simmering the pasta directly in the meat sauce, you get starch-infused richness with only one pan to wash. It is the ultimate fuss-free weeknight meal that tastes like it simmered for hours.

Weeknight cooking is a constant battle between wanting a deeply comforting, homemade meal and absolutely hating the mountain of dishes that usually accompanies it. Traditional pasta bakes are notorious offenders. You need a pot to boil the pasta, a colander to drain it, a skillet to brown the beef, and a baking dish to assemble the final casserole. By the time you sit down to eat, your sink is already overflowing.

This recipe changes that dynamic entirely. We are skipping the boiling step, the draining step, and the transfer step. By utilizing a single deep, oven-safe skillet, we brown the beef, simmer the pasta directly in its own self-thickening sauce, and melt a thick layer of cheese under the broiler. The starch released from the pasta as it cooks acts as a natural binder, yielding an ultra-velvety, luxurious texture without requiring heavy cream.

Beyond saving your sanity at the sink, this method ensures that every single tube of pasta is infused with savory beef flavor from the inside out. Instead of coating plain water-boiled pasta with sauce, the pasta drinks in the seasoned tomato broth as it swells. The result is a highly cohesive, protein-packed skillet bake that keeps beautifully for weekday lunches.

Key Takeaways

  • Total Time: 40 minutes from prep to table.
  • Difficulty: Very easy; beginner-friendly one-pan technique.
  • Protein Content: Over 30 grams of protein per serving.
  • Storage Life: Keeps exceptionally well in the fridge for up to 4 days.
  • Essential Equipment: A deep 12-inch oven-safe skillet (cast iron or stainless steel).

Recipe Card

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 25 minutes · Total: 40 minutes · Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb lean ground beef (90% lean or higher)
  • 8 oz uncooked rigatoni or penne pasta
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 can (15 oz) tomato sauce
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) petite diced tomatoes (undrained)
  • 2 cups low-sodium beef broth
  • 1.5 tsp dried Italian seasoning
  • 0.5 tsp smoked paprika
  • 0.5 tsp kosher salt
  • 0.25 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
  • 0.5 cup whole milk or half-and-half
  • 1.5 cups shredded low-moisture mozzarella cheese
  • 0.25 cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley (optional garnish)

Instructions

  1. Brown the Beef: Place a deep, 12-inch oven-safe skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it apart with a wooden spoon, for 5 to 6 minutes until no pink remains. If using a lower-fat beef, you do not need to drain. If excess grease accumulates, spoon out all but 1 tablespoon of fat.
  2. Sauté the Aromatics: Reduce the heat to medium. Stir in the diced yellow onion and minced garlic. Sauté for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring frequently, until the onions are translucent and fragrant.
  3. Toast the Tomato Paste: Clear a small space in the center of the skillet. Add the tomato paste and press it into the hot pan. Let it cook and darken slightly in color for 1 minute to caramelize the sugars and remove the raw metallic taste.
  4. Add Liquids and Seasonings: Pour in the tomato sauce, undrained diced tomatoes, beef broth, dried Italian seasoning, smoked paprika, kosher salt, and black pepper. Stir thoroughly to combine, scraping up any browned bits stuck to the bottom of the skillet.
  5. Simmer the Pasta: Stir the uncooked pasta directly into the liquid, pressing it down so it is mostly submerged. Bring the mixture to a boil, then immediately reduce the heat to medium-low. Cover the skillet with a tight-fitting lid and simmer for 12 to 14 minutes, stirring once or twice to prevent sticking, until the pasta is al dente and the liquid has reduced to a thick, rich sauce.
  6. Stir in the Cream: Remove the lid and turn off the heat. Pour in the milk or half-and-half, stirring gently until the sauce becomes a uniform, creamy orange-red color. Taste and adjust seasoning with additional salt or pepper if desired.
  7. Add the Cheese: Scatter the shredded mozzarella and grated Parmesan evenly over the top of the pasta.
  8. Broil and Melt: Preheat your oven's broiler to high. Transfer the skillet to the top rack of the oven (about 4 to 6 inches from the heat source). Broil for 3 to 4 minutes, watching closely, until the cheese is completely melted, bubbly, and speckled with golden-brown spots.
  9. Rest and Serve: Remove the skillet carefully from the oven (the handle will be extremely hot!). Let it rest for 5 minutes to allow the sauce to set slightly. Garnish with fresh chopped parsley and serve directly from the skillet.

Why the One-Skillet Pasta Method Works

Cooking pasta directly in its sauce is more than just a convenience hack; it is a fundamental culinary technique that yields superior results. When you boil pasta in a large pot of water, you discard the valuable starches that leach out into the liquid. In this one-pot creamy beef pasta bake, those starches are trapped inside the skillet. As the pasta cooks, it releases starch directly into the tomato broth and beef fat, emulsifying the mixture into a naturally creamy, glossy sauce that clings perfectly to every noodle.

Furthermore, because the pasta absorbs the seasoned beef broth and tomato juices rather than plain water, every single bite of rigatoni is packed with deep, savory flavor. This prevents the common issue found in traditional pasta bakes where the pasta tastes bland and separate from the sauce. For more information on the science of starch paste and starch gelatinization, you can read the Wikipedia article on starch gelatinization.

The Role of Acid and Fat Balance

A great pasta bake relies on balancing rich, heavy ingredients with bright, acidic ones. The acidity from the canned diced tomatoes and tomato paste cuts through the richness of the ground beef and melted mozzarella. Adding a splash of milk or half-and-half at the very end softens the sharp edges of the tomatoes, creating a rounded, comforting flavor profile that does not feel overly heavy on the palate.

Ingredient Notes and Smart Substitutions

This recipe is highly adaptable based on what you have in your pantry and refrigerator. Here is how to swap ingredients without compromising the final texture or cook times.

Choosing Your Pasta Shape

Short, sturdy pasta shapes with ridges are absolute non-negotiables for this recipe. The ridges act as tiny shelves that hold onto the thick, creamy beef sauce. Excellent choices include:

  • Rigatoni: Large, wide tubes that hold up beautifully to baking and provide a satisfying chew.
  • Penne Rigate: The classic choice; ensure you buy the ridged version (rigate) rather than smooth penne.
  • Cavatappi or Cellentani: Helical tubes that add a fun, bouncy texture to the dish.
  • Medium Shells: These act like tiny cups, scooping up pockets of meat and cheese.

Avoid long, delicate strands like spaghetti or angel hair, which will clump together and cook unevenly in a one-pot setup. Also, skip gluten-free pastas unless they are sturdy legume-based options (like chickpea pasta), as standard gluten-free rice-based pastas tend to break down and turn mushy when simmered directly in sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too much or too little liquid: One-pot pasta cooking is an exact science. If you add too much broth, you will end up with a watery soup. If you add too little, the pasta will remain chalky and hard. Stick strictly to the liquid ratios provided in the recipe card.
  • Not stirring during the simmer: Because you are cooking starch in a concentrated sauce, the pasta has a tendency to settle and stick to the bottom of the skillet. Be sure to stir the mixture thoroughly once or twice during the covered simmer to release any stuck pieces.
  • Using pre-shredded cheese from a bag: Pre-packaged shredded cheese is coated with anti-caking agents (like cellulose) to prevent it from clumping in the bag. These agents prevent the cheese from melting into a smooth, stretchy blanket. For the ultimate gooey cheese pull, buy a block of low-moisture mozzarella and grate it yourself.
  • Boiling the milk: Adding dairy to a boiling-hot, acidic tomato sauce can cause the milk proteins to curdle. Always turn off the heat before stirring in the milk or half-and-half to ensure a perfectly smooth, velvety finish.
  • Using a skillet that is too shallow: A standard skillet will overflow once you add the pasta and liquid. Use a deep-sided skillet (sometimes called a sauteuse or chef's pan) or a Dutch oven to ensure you have plenty of room to stir without spilling.

Storage, Reheating, and Make-Ahead Tips

This creamy beef pasta bake is a meal prep dream. Because the pasta is cooked directly in the sauce, it remains moist and flavorful even after a few days in the refrigerator.

How to Store Leftovers

Allow the pasta bake to cool completely to room temperature to prevent condensation from making the cheese soggy. Transfer the leftovers to an airtight container and store in the refrigerator for up to 4 days.

Reheating Instructions

As the pasta sits in the fridge, it will continue to absorb any excess moisture from the sauce. To restore its original creamy texture, use one of the following methods:

  • Microwave Method: Place a portion in a microwave-safe bowl. Splash 1 tablespoon of water or milk over the top to loosen the sauce. Cover with a damp paper towel and microwave on medium power in 1-minute increments, stirring in between, until heated through.
  • Oven Method: Place the leftovers in an oven-safe dish. Add a splash of broth or water, cover tightly with aluminum foil, and bake at 350°F (175°C) for 15 to 20 minutes until bubbling. Remove the foil for the last 5 minutes to let the cheese crisp up again.

Freezer Instructions

You can freeze this dish, though the texture of the pasta will be slightly softer upon thawing. To freeze, assemble the dish up to the point of adding the cheese (do not bake or broil). Let the mixture cool completely, transfer to a freezer-safe foil pan, top with the cheese, and wrap tightly with a double layer of plastic wrap and aluminum foil. Freeze for up to 3 months. To bake from frozen, remove the plastic wrap, cover with foil, and bake at 375°F (190°C) for 45 minutes. Remove the foil and bake for an additional 10 minutes until the cheese is melted and golden.

What to Serve with This Pasta Bake

Since this dish is incredibly rich and hearty, it pairs best with bright, crisp side dishes that cleanse the palate.

  • A Simple Green Salad: Toss crisp romaine or butter lettuce with a sharp, lemony vinaigrette to cut through the richness of the cheese and beef.
  • Roasted Broccoli or Broccolini: Tossed with olive oil, garlic, and a squeeze of fresh lemon juice, roasted green vegetables provide a lovely charred contrast to the soft pasta.
  • Garlic Bread: A classic pairing. Use a crusty baguette slathered in garlic butter to scoop up any leftover creamy sauce from your plate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make this recipe dairy-free?

Yes, you can easily adapt this dish to be dairy-free. Swap the whole milk for an unsweetened, unflavored plant-based milk (such as oat milk or cashew milk, which have a naturally creamy texture). Replace the mozzarella and Parmesan with your favorite dairy-free melting cheese alternatives, or omit the cheese topping entirely and finish the dish with a sprinkle of nutritional yeast for a savory, nutty kick.

My pasta is still hard but the liquid is gone. What do I do?

This can happen if your stove burner was set too high, causing the liquid to evaporate too quickly before the pasta had time to cook through. Simply stir in an extra 1/4 to 1/2 cup of beef broth or water, cover the skillet again, and continue simmering over low heat for an extra 3 to 4 minutes until the pasta is tender.

Can I use whole wheat pasta?

Whole wheat pasta can be used, but it requires slightly more liquid and a longer cooking time than white semolina pasta. If using whole wheat, increase the beef broth by 1/4 cup and expect to simmer the pasta for an additional 3 to 5 minutes. Keep a close eye on the skillet and add more liquid if the pan dries out before the pasta is cooked.

Do I have to use an oven-safe skillet?

If you do not own an oven-safe skillet, you can still make this recipe. Complete all the cooking steps on the stovetop. Once the pasta is cooked and you have stirred in the cream, transfer the entire mixture into a standard 9x13-inch baking dish. Top with the cheeses and place the baking dish under the broiler. This adds one extra dish to wash, but the final taste and texture will remain identical.

Is this recipe suitable for meal prep?

Absolutely. In fact, many home cooks find that the flavors meld and deepen after a night in the refrigerator. Divide the cooked and cooled pasta bake into individual, microwave-safe meal prep containers for easy, high-protein lunches throughout the week.

Can I add vegetables to this pasta bake?

Yes, this is an excellent way to sneek in extra nutrients. You can sauté diced bell peppers, zucchini, or mushrooms along with the onions and garlic. Alternatively, you can stir in a few handfuls of fresh baby spinach right at the end when you add the milk; the residual heat will wilt the spinach perfectly in seconds.

This one-pot creamy beef pasta bake proves that you do not need to sacrifice flavor or texture to enjoy a quick, low-cleanup weeknight dinner. By letting the pasta cook directly in the savory beef and tomato sauce, you unlock a depth of flavor and a silky texture that traditional boiling methods simply cannot match. Gather your ingredients, dust off your favorite skillet, and enjoy a comforting, high-protein meal that the whole family will love.
